# Added literature: game-inspired features and citizen science

These 15 references supplement the [initial evidence inventory](feature_evidence_inventory.md). They support the thesis on **engagement, learning and data utility**, with active participation as one engagement measure. Checked 17 November 2026. This is a curated reading list, not a systematic review or completed full-text synthesis.

Bibliographic metadata was checked against the DOI registry. Metadata verification does not verify a paper’s findings. Original supplied titles, dates, links and summaries are preserved in the [structured register](literature_register.json), explicitly labeled as user notes. No publisher PDFs are redistributed.

Citation years prefer print/issue publication when available. Bonney et al. U06 appeared online in 2015 and in print in 2016; Pernat et al. U15 appeared online in 2020 and in print in 2021. Both dates remain in the register.

## References and preliminary screening

### U01: Bonney et al. (2014)

**Next Steps for Citizen Science**. Science. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1251554); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1126%2Fscience.1251554).

- Relevance: Background; all three outcomes.
- Design / role: General citizen-science agenda.
- Working summary: Use as framing for citizen-science research, not as a game-feature impact estimate.
- Review status: Metadata checked; detailed evidence screening pending.

### U02: Eveleigh et al. (2013)

**“I want to be a captain! I want to be a captain!”: gamification in the old weather citizen science project**. Proceedings of the First International Conference on Gameful Design, Research, and Applications. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2583008.2583019); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F2583008.2583019).

- Relevance: Engagement; Old Weather (Zooniverse).
- Design / role: Survey and interviews about competitive ranks.
- Working summary: Ranks motivated some participants, while others ignored them or disengaged. Project-specific qualitative evidence does not establish a platform-wide causal effect.
- Review status: Primary abstract and author manuscript abstract checked; sample and detailed extraction pending.

### U03: Aristeidou et al. (2017)

**Profiles of engagement in online communities of citizen science participation**. Computers in Human Behavior. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2017.04.044); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016%2Fj.chb.2017.04.044).

- Relevance: Engagement; online citizen science.
- Design / role: Engagement profiles.
- Working summary: Potential framework for distinguishing patterns of participation. The supplied description of logs and questionnaires still needs independent methods extraction.
- Review status: Metadata checked; outcome and methods verification pending.

### U04: Newman et al. (2012)

**The future of citizen science: emerging technologies and shifting paradigms**. Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1890/110294); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1890%2F110294).

- Relevance: Background; technology and participation.
- Design / role: Conceptual overview.
- Working summary: Use to contextualize emerging technologies and citizen-science practices, not to estimate a game-feature effect.
- Review status: Metadata checked; detailed evidence screening pending.

### U05: See et al. (2016)

**Crowdsourcing, Citizen Science or Volunteered Geographic Information? The Current State of Crowdsourced Geographic Information**. ISPRS International Journal of Geo-Information. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi5050055); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390%2Fijgi5050055).

- Relevance: Data utility; geographic citizen science.
- Design / role: Review and terminology.
- Working summary: Useful context for crowdsourced geographic information and data-quality questions; applicability to the four platforms needs explicit justification.
- Review status: Metadata checked; detailed evidence screening pending.

### U06: Bonney et al. (2016)

**Can citizen science enhance public understanding of science?**. Public Understanding of Science. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963662515607406); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177%2F0963662515607406).

- Relevance: Learning; citizen science broadly.
- Design / role: Review.
- Working summary: Relevant to public understanding of science. Extract the underlying study designs before making a learning-impact claim.
- Review status: Metadata checked; detailed evidence extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: 2015 online publication and 2016 print issue; use 2016 as the citation year and retain both dates.

### U07: Curtis (2015)

**Motivation to Participate in an Online Citizen Science Game: A Study of Foldit**. Science Communication. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1075547015609322); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177%2F1075547015609322).

- Relevance: Engagement; Foldit.
- Design / role: Survey, interviews and participant observation.
- Working summary: Study of 37 players identifies scientific contribution, interest, social interaction and intellectual challenge as motivations. It does not test whether redesign causes better retention or data quality.
- Review status: Primary abstract checked; full-text extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: Correct DOI is 10.1177/1075547015609322; supplied DOI omitted the final 2.
- Correction / caution: Single author Vickie Curtis, rather than Curtis et al.

### U08: Curtis (2014)

**Online citizen science games: Opportunities for the biological sciences**. Applied &amp; Translational Genomics. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atg.2014.07.001); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016%2Fj.atg.2014.07.001).

- Relevance: Background; biological citizen-science games.
- Design / role: Review.
- Working summary: Discusses biological citizen-science games including Foldit, Phylo and Eterna, with research and informal-learning opportunities. Potential benefits are not measured treatment effects.
- Review status: Primary abstract checked; full-text extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: Single author Vickie Curtis, rather than Curtis et al.
- Correction / caution: Supplied summary appears mismatched to this games review; retain as an unverified user note.

### U09: Miller et al. (2019)

**Expertise and engagement: re-designing citizen science games with players' minds in mind**. Proceedings of the 14th International Conference on the Foundations of Digital Games. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3337722.3337735); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F3337722.3337735).

- Relevance: Engagement and progression; Foldit.
- Design / role: Tutorial interface and mechanics comparisons.
- Working summary: Examines six tutorial changes informed by cognitive load and self-determination theories. Abstract reports a level-selection interface effect on progression and a relationship between expertise and engagement; do not infer that every change improved all outcomes.
- Review status: Primary author abstract checked; allocation, sample, estimates and uncertainty pending.
- Correction / caution: Supplied summary overgeneralizes benefits; extract results separately for each intervention and outcome.

### U10: Cooper et al. (2018)

**Repurposing citizen science games as software tools for professional scientists**. Proceedings of the 13th International Conference on the Foundations of Digital Games. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3235765.3235770); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F3235765.3235770).

- Relevance: Software utility; Foldit and EyeWire.
- Design / role: Professional-tool repurposing case studies.
- Working summary: FolditStandalone and EyeWire illustrate reuse as professional tools. Software usefulness must be distinguished from validated improvements in citizen-generated data quality.
- Review status: Primary abstract checked; full-text extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: Publication year is 2018, rather than supplied 2020.
- Correction / caution: Professional software utility does not by itself demonstrate improved citizen-science data quality.

### U11: Miller et al. (2023)

**Wrapped in Story: The Affordances of Narrative for Citizen Science Games**. Proceedings of the 18th International Conference on the Foundations of Digital Games. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3582437.3582443); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F3582437.3582443).

- Relevance: Engagement; Foldit narrative.
- Design / role: Narrative tutorial comparison.
- Working summary: Abstract reports more time in tutorial and science puzzles, without a substantial change in tutorial progress. Time spent is not by itself demonstrated learning or data utility.
- Review status: Primary abstract checked; allocation, sample and effect estimates pending.

### U12: Miller et al. (2022)

**A Survey of Citizen Science Gaming Experiences**. Citizen Science: Theory and Practice. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.5334/cstp.500); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.5334%2Fcstp.500).

- Relevance: Engagement and design; multiple citizen-science games.
- Design / role: Player survey and qualitative content analysis.
- Working summary: Survey of 185 players across nine games identifies experiences with scientific communication, instruction, controls, tasks and software. It describes player reports rather than isolating feature effects.
- Review status: Primary article/manuscript abstract checked; full-text extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: Publication year is 2022, rather than supplied 2023.

### U13: Milburn et al. (2023)

**Join the Fold: Video Games, Science Fiction, and the Refolding of Citizen Science**. Design Issues. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/desi_a_00707); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1162%2Fdesi_a_00707).

- Relevance: Background; narrative and citizen-science games.
- Design / role: Narrative and cultural design perspective.
- Working summary: Use for the conceptual role of narrative and science fiction. Keep distinct from the Foldit narrative outcome study U11.
- Review status: Metadata checked; detailed evidence screening pending.

### U14: Speelman et al. (2023)

**Serious games and citizen science; from parallel pathways to greater synergies**. Current Opinion in Environmental Sustainability. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cosust.2023.101320); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016%2Fj.cosust.2023.101320).

- Relevance: Background; serious games and citizen science.
- Design / role: Conceptual overview.
- Working summary: Discusses synergies in participatory environmental management. The supplied claim about declining interest curves needs full-text support before use.
- Review status: Primary institutional abstract checked; full-text extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: Do not use the supplied interest-curve claim without checking the full text.

### U15: Pernat et al. (2021)

**Citizen science versus professional data collection: Comparison of approaches to mosquito monitoring in Germany**. Journal of Applied Ecology. [Paper / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.13767); [registry metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111%2F1365-2664.13767).

- Relevance: Data utility; mosquito monitoring outside core platforms.
- Design / role: Citizen versus professional collection comparison.
- Working summary: Citizen reports and professional trapping provide complementary information with different sampling biases. This is a data-fitness example, not a gamification intervention.
- Review status: Primary abstract checked; full-text extraction pending.
- Correction / caution: 2020 online publication and 2021 print issue; use 2021 as the citation year and retain both dates.

## Extraction still needed

For outcome studies, extract comparison conditions, allocation, sample and attrition, instruments, effect estimates and uncertainty, follow-up, and confounders before making impact claims. Separate tutorial progression from measured learning, time spent from retention, software reuse from validated data quality, and qualitative motivation from causal effects. General reviews and the mosquito study provide context; they do not estimate game-feature effects across Eterna, Zooniverse, EyeWire and Foldit.
